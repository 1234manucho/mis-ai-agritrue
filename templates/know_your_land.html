From information to action

Use land information as a starting point—not the final diagnosis.

Good farm decisions combine local data, field observation, testing and experienced agricultural advice.

Understand the county context

Review the available soil, weather and crop information to understand the general farming context in your area.

Test your actual field

Use a soil test to confirm field-specific pH, nutrients and fertilizer requirements before investing in major inputs.

Confirm important decisions

Consult an extension officer, agronomist, laboratory or other qualified professional when the decision could materially affect your farm.

Farmer field habits

Build a better picture of your land over time.

Simple observations collected consistently can reveal seasonal changes that a single snapshot may miss.

Create a simple field map

Mark wet areas, slopes, compacted sections, crop performance and visible soil differences so you can compare zones within your farm.

Keep a farm journal

Record planting dates, rainfall, inputs, pests, symptoms and harvest results. These records improve future planning and professional consultations.

Photograph the same location

Take regular photographs from the same point to compare crop growth, erosion, vegetation cover and seasonal changes.

Interactive reference

Explore the Kenya soils map

Use the interactive map as an additional reference for broader soil patterns. Zoom and pan to inspect different areas.
